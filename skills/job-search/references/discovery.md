# Adaptive discovery and search hygiene

Read for initial source planning and recurring discovery. Stay inside the candidate-approved occupation, geography, working arrangement and exclusions. Broader vocabulary expands discovery, not the candidate's claimed experience or authorized search scope.

## Learn useful titles

Maintain a private `notes/title-aliases.json` ledger with alias, role family, example posting source, duty signal, first/last observed times, and active/selective/retired status. Learn aliases from real inspected postings, including regional spelling, comma-inverted titles, junior/senior abbreviations and numbered tiers. Record why an alias is selective or retired. A title's appearance alone does not justify adopting unsupported seniority.

Rotate at least four query clusters when the agreed time/source budget permits: direct titles, employer title variants, evidence-supported duty phrases independent of titles, and adjacent family or specialist/public-sector sources. If scope supports fewer clusters, record why; do not invent unrelated titles to fill a quota. Over consecutive bounded runs, rotate deferred clusters. Keep useful zero-result queries with exact query, source, scope, time and pagination inspected so the next run learns rather than repeats blindly.

Include province/state and country when a city is ambiguous. Count raw hits separately from plausible distinct postings and inspected/qualified leads. “Exhausted” applies only to a recorded query/source and inspected pagination, never the whole market.

## Verify and classify

A cached snippet or redirect is not proof an opening remains available. An empty ATS extraction is not proof it closed: inspect the actual page with an available permitted method, try an official alternative, or mark verification blocked. Save observed posting status and evidence; do not infer rejection from posting closure.

Before tailoring, record strong, selective or excluded in the lead's private screening note, with central duties, mandatory/equivalent/preferred requirements, supporting fact IDs and the reason. Strong means the central work and mandatory route are evidenced; selective means a credible core with a material uncertainty or preferred gap; excluded means an evidenced hard mismatch or a candidate exclusion.

Apply unwanted-duty exclusions when that duty is the role's dominant purpose, not merely mentioned. Let the candidate define exclusions; inherit none from the author's occupation or personal preferences. A preferred proprietary platform is not automatically disqualifying. Higher pay does not justify a speculative application whose core requirements lack evidence. A selective lead can justify one useful question before a final screening decision.

## Platform rules

Use employer pages, native alerts and permitted feeds/APIs or ordinary allowed browsing. Follow each site's applicable terms and access controls; if a method is restricted or blocked, use a permitted route or report the limitation. Do not bulk auto-submit, defeat CAPTCHA/access controls, spoof fingerprints, rotate proxies to evade restrictions or scrape against site terms. Alerts can arrive late; applicant counters are not hiring probability.

Save discovery source separately from application channel: a role found through a board may be applied to on the employer ATS. Preserve observed query/source history even when the final application URL differs. Complete one application through final review before starting the next when deadlines permit; persist employer-confirmed submission records immediately before advancing the queue.
